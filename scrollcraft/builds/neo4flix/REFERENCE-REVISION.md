# Reference-led motion revision

The user rejected the earlier subtle poster treatment and supplied a 59.9-second video. This is the new design reference. It shows a bike rotating against an independent landscape, a landscape becoming a room window, a moving dimensional waveform, a can rotating and travelling between panels, and separate foreground/background layers around a model. The significant properties are large spatial travel, real object rotation, clear occlusion, and scene-scale camera transitions. A small card reveal does not reproduce them.

The prior authored creative direction is retained only for the product identity. The new scene is a physical cinema reel, a perforated film strip, and a cinematic landscape. It appears on the public entrance and the authenticated Discover page.

Feeling curve before choreography: anticipation from a large lit reel already moving on entry; discovery as the reel rotates and the film strip crosses the scene; immersion as the camera enters the central aperture; agency when the catalogue becomes the destination. The peak is the camera travelling through the reel, not the old poster fan.

Layer contract: a generated landscape clean plate at the back, studio-lit metal reel and film strip in actual WebGL space, near-frame perforations passing the camera, and readable HTML type on independent planes. Text remains usable while decorative objects change. The opening, middle and exit must each look substantially different in captured pixels.

The page-local renderer is shared with Angular and has explicit disposal. The original ScrollCraft engine remains unchanged and serves the remaining entrance sections. Normal motion remains available on phones, with a shorter camera path and portrait composition. Only reduced-motion or unavailable WebGL uses the static scene. The film catalogue remains reachable directly without watching the sequence.

Static visual fallback must be captured from the final live scene. Evidence must include motion samples or a short recording, large pixel changes between phases, mobile motion, reduced-motion handling, WebGL failure, and existing account features.
